## Overview

Chile is a small-emitter country (~105 MtCO2e, roughly 0.25% of historical global emissions) with outsized leverage over the clean-energy transition. The central valley and Atacama desert sit on the world's largest reserves of copper and the second-largest reserves of lithium — together the metallic backbone of the electric vehicle supply chain. How Chile manages those resources will shape whether the global EV pivot delivers decarbonization without exporting ecological damage to the salt flats of the high Andes.

The domestic environmental picture is genuinely bifurcated. The electricity grid is now 68% renewable (Ember 2025), up 22 percentage points in five years, and solar generation in the Atacama — the most irradiated place on Earth — routinely clears prices to zero at midday. Coal has fallen from 40% of power in 2018 to 14%, on track for full phase-out by 2040 (accelerated from the original 2050 schedule). The 2022 Climate Change Framework Law was the first of its kind in South America, codifying net zero by 2050 with binding carbon budgets and sectoral targets. The 2022 Water Code reform reclassified water as a public good, unwinding a Pinochet-era market-based regime that is widely seen as having enabled agricultural over-extraction during a 15-year megadrought. Chile ratified the Escazú Agreement in 2022, becoming the regional standard-bearer for environmental democracy.

At the same time, central Chile is entering the sixteenth year of the longest drought in instrumental record. Catastrophic fires in 2023, 2024, and 2025 burned over 700,000 hectares combined, killing more than 150 people across the three seasons and exposing the vulnerability of monoculture pine and eucalyptus plantations that cover 2.4 million hectares. Salmon aquaculture — Chile is the world's #2 producer — is pushing into Patagonian fjords and Kawésqar National Reserve, with chronic sea-lice, antibiotic, and escape problems. Sacrifice zones at Quintero-Puchuncaví, Huasco, and Coronel concentrate heavy-metal and SO2 pollution near low-income communities. The 2022-2023 constitutional rewrite that would have enshrined rights of nature and indigenous autonomy was rejected at two consecutive plebiscites, leaving structural reform incomplete.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Emissions trajectory vs 1.5°C pathway (32% gap).** Current policies put Chile at ~102 MtCO2e in 2030; a 1.5°C-compatible pathway is closer to ~77 MtCO2e. Closing this requires electrifying mining haul trucks and industrial heat, cutting diesel transport, and protecting the LULUCF sink that fires are eroding.

**2. Coal exit vs grid reality (14% vs 0%).** The 2040 phase-out is credible on capacity but northern mining load — the heaviest grid demand — is still partly met by coal. Accelerating to a 2030 exit would require purpose-built transmission from southern wind and central solar to the Atacama mining belt.

**3. Terrestrial protected areas (21.3% vs 30%).** Marine coverage at 41.8% is world-class, but terrestrial 30×30 still requires ~7 Mha of additions, most credibly in the coastal range and central-valley Mediterranean biome where endemism is highest and formal protection is thinnest.

**4. Salar de Atacama water budget.** Lithium brine extraction plus freshwater pumping by SQM and Albemarle removes ~1,700 L/s from an already stressed salt-flat system. Lickanantay communities have documented measurable aquifer decline. The Codelco-SQM joint venture signed in 2025 commits to direct lithium extraction technology by 2030, but the transition window is where the ecological damage accumulates.

**5. Native forest vs plantation economics.** Chile has lost ~800,000 hectares of native forest since 2000, mostly replaced by radiata pine and eucalyptus plantations that burn hotter, sequester less carbon long-term, and host minimal biodiversity. Reversing this requires restructuring the Decree 701 subsidy legacy that built the plantation economy.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **March 2026:** Codelco-SQM Salar de Atacama JV with direct-lithium-extraction commitment by 2030.
- **February 2026:** Second NDC retains absolute 95 MtCO2e cap, adds methane sub-target and NBS accounting.
- **November 2025:** Ventanas copper smelter permanently closed, ending 60 years of SO2 emissions in Quintero-Puchuncaví.
- **September 2025:** Environmental Tribunal suspends six salmon farms in Kawésqar Reserve for oxygen-depletion violations.
- **January 2026:** Dorotea-Yendegaia protected corridor extended, connecting 1.1 Mha of Patagonian rewilding landscape.

**Negative:**

- **August 2025:** ~180,000 ha burned in central-south megafires, 17 deaths — third consecutive catastrophic fire season.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Chile closed its top gaps, it would:

- Tighten the 2030 cap from 95 to ~77 MtCO2e to align with 1.5°C, and accelerate the coal exit from 2040 to 2032 by building dedicated renewable transmission to the northern mining belt.
- Mandate direct lithium extraction (DLE) industry-wide by 2028 rather than 2030, with binding aquifer-level thresholds co-governed with Lickanantay communities.
- Add ~7 Mha of terrestrial protected area in the coastal range and central-valley biomes where endemism is highest, moving from 21.3% to 30%.
- Restructure plantation subsidies to favor native-forest restoration, with a fuel-load management program that treats fire as a planning constraint rather than an emergency.
- Extend Escazú implementation with a dedicated Mapuche defender-protection protocol in Araucanía, where Global Witness continues to record killings.
- Finish the constitutional loop on rights of nature and water as human right through targeted legislation, given the failure of the two plebiscites.

## Data Confidence

**High.** Emissions, energy, protected areas, and governance indicators are multi-source and consistent. Biodiversity and agriculture rely partly on single-source assessments (IUCN, FAO). Defender and indigenous-land-recognition data are likely undercounts, though less so than in countries without Escazú ratification.

## Key Tension

Chile's clean-energy grid and rewilding leadership run on the same balance sheet as a lithium and copper mining boom that drains Atacama aquifers — the country is powering the global EV transition while watching its own water table fall.
