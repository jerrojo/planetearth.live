# Russia — Planet Lens Profile

**Composite Score: 30 / 100 — RED**
**Last updated: 2026-04-20**

---

## Overview

Russia is a planetary-scale environmental actor whose recent trajectory has been sharply negative. It spans eleven time zones and contains roughly a fifth of the world's forests, the largest single expanse of boreal taiga on Earth, and more than half the planet's permafrost. Those endowments make Russia structurally indispensable to any viable climate future. The Arctic sections of the country are warming at roughly four times the global average, and permafrost thaw is already releasing methane and destabilizing pipelines, railways, and cities built on frozen ground. At the same time, Russia remains the world's fourth-largest annual greenhouse gas emitter at about 2.58 GtCO2e and among the highest per-capita at ~17.8 tCO2e — figures that reflect an economy and state budget still organized around oil, gas, and coal extraction.

The post-2022 period has re-entrenched that orientation. Sanctions redirected hydrocarbon exports toward Asia rather than curbing them, war-driven fiscal needs elevated fossil rents to roughly 13% of GDP, and the Strategy for Low Carbon Development to 2050 adopted in 2021 has been quietly shelved. The international scaffolding that once disciplined Russian environmental performance — Council of Europe membership, Bern Convention, full Arctic Council cooperation, EU partnerships on methane MRV — has been dismantled or frozen. Domestic civil society that once provided independent monitoring has been hollowed out: Greenpeace Russia was declared 'undesirable' in 2023, Bellona's Russian arm dissolved, and WWF Russia was forced to wind down in 2025. The result is not only a worsening environmental record but a darker and patchier evidence base for observing it.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Emissions vs 1.5°C pathway (~96% gap).** Under current policies, Russia is projected to emit ~2.45 GtCO2e in 2030. A 1.5°C-compatible path would require roughly 1.25 GtCO2e. The official NDC — 30% below 1990 — is a non-constraint because the 1990s industrial collapse already delivered most of that cut; the pledge functions as a ceiling well above the trajectory. Closing the real gap would require an absolute, forward-looking target and binding sectoral caps that do not yet exist in law.

**2. Fossil share of electricity (61% vs <25% needed).** Russia's grid is dominated by gas (~46%), followed by nuclear (~19%), hydro (~17%), and coal (~17%), with wind and solar combined below 1%. The 2060 net-zero declaration implies a near-total decarbonization of power in 35 years, but renewable deployment has averaged about 1 percentage point of growth over five years — roughly an order of magnitude too slow. Nuclear expansion provides a low-carbon anchor but cannot by itself close the gap.

**3. Forest and permafrost integrity.** The taiga has historically been a 500–600 MtCO2/yr sink. Global Forest Watch data show accelerating tree-cover loss, and the 2021 and 2023 Siberian wildfire seasons were among the largest on record. Peer-reviewed studies now suggest the sink has collapsed or flipped to a source in some regions once fires and permafrost emissions are accounted for. Because Russia's NDC leans heavily on the "absorptive capacity of forests and other ecosystems", a weakening sink is a hidden solvency problem for the entire pledge.

**4. Protected areas (13.5% terrestrial / 3.5% marine vs 30%).** The zapovednik network of strict reserves, inherited from 1916, is globally distinctive but insufficient in area. Recent legislative changes make it easier to redraw PA boundaries around 'strategic' oil, gas, and transport projects, moving the trend line in the wrong direction at the exact moment the GBF requires expansion.

**5. Environmental governance and civic space.** Independent NGOs, investigative journalism on pollution, and academic collaboration with Western monitoring networks have all contracted sharply since 2022. Foreign-agent, undesirable-organization, and discreditation laws make routine environmental reporting legally risky. This is itself an environmental outcome: without independent observers, oil spills, permafrost failures, and illegal logging are slower to surface and harder to contest.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Negative:**

- **December 2025:** Federal law expanded the grounds for reducing protected-area boundaries to accommodate strategic infrastructure, with explicit relevance to Arctic oil and gas.
- **September 2025:** WWF Russia forced to fully dissolve after 'undesirable' designation; long-running Amur tiger and leopard monitoring transferred to state bodies with reduced transparency.
- **July 2025:** Ministry of Natural Resources reported over 9 million hectares burned through mid-summer in another near-record Siberian fire season.
- **April 2025:** Russia refused to participate in the EU's revised methane reporting framework for gas imports, following its broader withdrawal from climate MRV partnerships.

**Positive:**

- **June 2025:** Sakhalin regional carbon trading experiment extended to 2028 with first compliance-cycle results published — still the only operational ETS in Russia.
- **February 2026:** Rosatom commissioned additional reactor units, sustaining nuclear at roughly 19% of electricity.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Russia chose to close its top gaps, it would:

- Replace the 30%-below-1990 NDC with an absolute 2030 and 2035 target built forward from current emissions, and enshrine the 2060 net-zero goal in binding legislation.
- Set a hard declining cap on coal generation post-2028 and a sectoral budget for gas, backed by federal transfers to energy-dependent regions (Kuzbass, Khanty-Mansi, Sakhalin).
- Re-open independent environmental monitoring: restore NGO operating space, re-join Arctic science networks, and re-engage international MRV partnerships.
- Invest in fire management, indigenous-led land stewardship, and peatland and permafrost protection as the cheapest near-term climate levers available anywhere.
- Expand the zapovednik and national park systems toward 30x30, and end the practice of redrawing PA boundaries for extractive projects.
- Publish an Arctic adaptation plan that treats permafrost thaw as the infrastructure and public-safety problem it already is.

## Data Confidence

**Medium.** Emissions, electricity-mix, and protected-area figures are high-confidence from multiple independent sources (OWID, Ember, Protected Planet, CAT). Biodiversity, forest-sink, and agriculture indicators are more fragile: the collapse of cross-border scientific cooperation and the shutdown of independent Russian NGOs have thinned the data pipelines that used to feed the IUCN Red List and remote-sensing ground truth. The defenders-killed figure of zero almost certainly reflects a reporting void rather than safety for environmental activists.

## Key Tension

The country holding the world's largest boreal carbon sink and a warming Arctic has rebuilt its state budget around fossil exports and dismantled the civil-society and international scaffolding that would let anyone verify what is happening to its forests, permafrost, and emissions.
