## Overview

South Africa is the continent's most ambitious environmental legislator and its most coal-dependent economy at the same time. Roughly 82% of electricity still comes from the Eskom coal fleet concentrated on the Mpumalanga Highveld, and that fleet drives about 78% of national emissions — ~442 MtCO2e in 2025, a per-capita footprint of 7.1 tonnes that is the highest in Africa and higher than many European economies. The headline emissions number has fallen ~9% over the decade, but that decline is not a virtuous transition story: it reflects the collapse of Eskom's energy availability factor during the load-shedding years of 2022–2024. The country effectively decarbonised by failing to keep the lights on. In 2025 the grid stabilised — over 330 consecutive loadshedding-free days — and emissions ticked back up.

Against that backdrop, the policy architecture is unusually serious. The July 2024 Climate Change Act is the first binding national climate law in Africa, establishing sectoral carbon budgets with legal teeth and putting the Presidential Climate Commission on statutory footing. Phase 2 of the Carbon Tax begins in 2026 with a headline rate of R236/tCO2e, tapering free allowances for the first time. A 2022 Constitutional Court ruling in the "Deadly Air" case found that the government's failure to enforce air-quality standards on the Highveld violated constitutional rights — a landmark decision globally. And South Africa is one of 17 megadiverse countries, custodian of the Cape Floristic Region (one of only six floral kingdoms on Earth), the succulent Karoo, three biodiversity hotspots, and iconic megafauna.

The tension is that the Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP) — the $8.5B (now $11.8B pledged, $9.3B after US withdrawal in late 2025) climate-finance deal announced at COP26 — has delivered only a fraction of promised capital, most of it as loans rather than grants, and Mpumalanga's coal regions are politically and economically unprepared for managed decline. The ANC–DA Government of National Unity formed after the 2024 election has not yet resolved whether the transition accelerates or stalls.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Coal share of electricity (82% vs <30% needed).** This is the defining gap. South Africa's 1.5°C pathway requires coal below 30% of electricity by 2030; the current trajectory under IRP 2025, which extended rather than accelerated retirements, gets to roughly 60%. The bottleneck is less technical than political: Mpumalanga's coal communities represent ~100,000 direct jobs, and no credible reskilling or local-economy plan has been funded at scale.

**2. 2030 emissions vs 1.5°C pathway (37% gap).** Current policies project ~410 MtCO2e in 2030, above the NDC midpoint and well above a 1.5°C-compatible ~300 MtCO2e. Closing this gap requires Eskom and Sasol (Secunda, the world's largest point-source CO2 emitter) to follow binding decarbonisation trajectories — both are beginning to be priced through Carbon Tax Phase 2 but not yet forced.

**3. Terrestrial protected areas (9.24% vs 30%).** Kruger, iSimangaliso and Table Mountain are world-class, but grassland and succulent Karoo biomes are severely under-protected, and ecological connectivity between protected fragments is weak. The marine figure (14.55%) is stronger after the 2019 MPA expansion.

**4. Highveld air pollution (PM2.5 ~5× WHO guideline).** Despite the 2022 Deadly Air constitutional ruling, enforcement remains patchy. Eskom repeatedly applies for exemptions from Minimum Emission Standards on the grounds of grid security. A generation of children in the coal belt is growing up with measurable respiratory impact.

**5. JETP financing delivered vs pledged (~82% shortfall).** The political cost of JETP's failure to mobilise at scale is a collapse of trust — both within South Africa, where unions and coal-region communities now frame JETP as a Northern imposition, and globally, where JETP was meant to be the template for Indonesia, Vietnam, Senegal and beyond.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **July 2024:** Climate Change Act signed into law — binding sectoral carbon budgets and a statutory Presidential Climate Commission.
- **June 2025:** Loadshedding suspended 330+ consecutive days; ~6 GW of private rooftop solar now on the grid.
- **February 2026:** Carbon Tax Phase 2 begins at R236/tCO2e, with free allowances tapering — the first binding cost signal on Eskom and Sasol.

**Negative:**

- **September 2025:** IRP 2025 extends Komati and Camden coal retirements, citing grid security.
- **November 2025:** US withdraws from JETP; pledge falls from $11.8B to $9.3B, grant share shrinks further.
- **January 2026:** Rhino poaching reported at 420 animals in 2024 — fourth consecutive annual rise in KwaZulu-Natal.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If South Africa closed its top gaps, it would:

- Commit to a binding coal retirement schedule — not a capacity ceiling — taking the fleet below 50% of electricity by 2030 and under 20% by 2035, with funded reskilling and local-economy plans for Mpumalanga attached to each closure date.
- Land the NDC at the lower bound (350 Mt) and align Carbon Tax Phase 3 with that pathway, removing remaining free allowances for liquid-fuels and power by 2030.
- Expand terrestrial protected areas from 9.24% toward 30% by formally recognising communal conservancies and OECMs, particularly in the succulent Karoo and grassland biomes currently under-represented.
- Enforce Minimum Emission Standards on the Highveld with no further Eskom exemptions, backed by the 2022 Deadly Air judgment.
- Restructure JETP into a majority-grant facility, ringfenced for just-transition spending in coal regions, with transparent quarterly reporting.
- Scale up the Working for Water / Working on Fire model — ecological restoration paid as public employment — from hundreds of millions of rand to tens of billions, as the flagship climate-jobs programme.

## Data Confidence

**Medium.** Emissions, energy, protected-area and air-quality data are high-confidence (Eskom, Ember, Protected Planet, NASA satellite all converge). Biodiversity pillar draws on strong SANBI science. Fossil subsidy and defenders figures rely on cross-country databases with known reporting gaps; indigenous-land-recognition figure reflects contested Khoi-San land-claim processes still in motion.

## Key Tension

South Africa has Africa's most developed climate law and the continent's most coal-dependent grid in the same country — the Just Energy Transition is a test case for whether an emerging economy can decarbonise without deepening unemployment in regions whose entire economy is built on coal.
