## Overview

New Zealand / Aotearoa has a brand built on clean green mountains, glacier-fed rivers, and the "100% Pure" tourism pitch. The underlying environmental ledger is messier. At roughly 76 MtCO2e of gross emissions, Aotearoa is a small emitter in absolute terms — about 0.1% of the cumulative global total since 1850 — but per-capita emissions of around 14.6 tCO2e place it among the dirtiest economies in the OECD on a per-person basis. The unusual shape of that footprint is the story: roughly half of New Zealand's greenhouse gas emissions come from agriculture, chiefly enteric methane from 26 million sheep and 10 million dairy and beef cattle. No other high-income country is this dependent on ruminants. That is why the Climate Change Response (Zero Carbon) Amendment Act of 2019 — a genuinely progressive piece of legislation that put net-zero by 2050 into law — had to carve biogenic methane out of the main target, settling for a weaker 24–47% reduction trajectory that the dairy lobby has been trying to soften ever since.

Biodiversity is the other defining feature. Eighty million years of isolation produced an archipelago of flightless birds, ancient reptiles, and endemic plants with few parallels anywhere on Earth. Introduced mammals — stoats, possums, rats — have been a four-century-long ecological catastrophe, and roughly 74% of native terrestrial birds are now threatened or at risk. The Predator Free 2050 programme, launched in 2016, is the world's most ambitious national eradication effort, and in 2025 it produced the first sustained increase in kiwi numbers in a century. That success, however, sits alongside kauri dieback still advancing through northern forests and the continued collapse of freshwater ecosystems downstream of intensive dairy.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Emissions trajectory vs 1.5°C pathway (~52% gap).** Under current policies New Zealand is projected to emit around 64 MtCO2e in 2030 domestically. A CAT-compatible 1.5°C pathway puts that closer to 42 Mt. The country leans heavily on purchasing offshore mitigation to bridge its 2030 NDC rather than cutting gross emissions at home — a posture flagged as high-risk by the Climate Change Commission.

**2. Agricultural methane (49% of emissions, pricing deferred to 2030).** Until livestock sit inside a binding emissions price, the largest wedge of the national footprint is effectively unregulated. He Waka Eke Noa — the farmer-designed pricing partnership — collapsed in 2023, and the current coalition government has deferred ETS entry to 2030 and opened the methane target itself to review.

**3. Strict marine protection (<1% no-take outside Kermadec).** The headline figure of ~30% marine protection is inflated by the remote Kermadec / Rangitāhua region. Around New Zealand's productive mainland coast, fully protected no-take reserves cover under 1% of the EEZ, well below what the Global Biodiversity Framework asks of wealthy maritime nations.

**4. Freshwater quality (~45% of rivers unswimmable).** Decades of dairy intensification, nitrogen fertilizer (tripled since 1990), and sediment loss have degraded lowland rivers and aquifers. Canterbury and Southland have nitrate levels in drinking water that exceed precautionary health thresholds. The 2020 Te Mana o te Wai framework was partially unwound in 2024.

**5. Fossil-sector policy reversal.** Direct fossil fuel subsidies are small in dollar terms (~NZD 0.5B), but the December 2024 repeal of the offshore exploration ban, combined with fast-track consents for iron-sand seabed mining, represents a policy reversal with outsized signaling value for a country that once led the High Ambition Coalition.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **December 2024:** 2035 NDC filed at UNFCCC — 51–55% cut below 2005 gross emissions by 2035.
- **June 2025:** DOC reported kiwi numbers up to ~70,000 birds — first sustained increase in a century under Predator Free 2050.
- **February 2026:** Supreme Court allowed *Smith v Fonterra* climate tort case to proceed to full trial, opening a novel corporate-liability front.

**Negative:**

- **December 2024:** Crown Minerals Amendment Bill passed, reversing the 2018 offshore oil & gas exploration ban.
- **October 2025:** National Policy Statement for Freshwater Management weakened — nitrogen caps and winter-grazing rules loosened.
- **August 2025:** Fast-track Approvals Act used to consent Trans-Tasman Resources' seabed iron-sand mining project off Taranaki over iwi and ecological objections.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Aotearoa closed its top gaps, it would:

- Bring agricultural emissions under a binding price well before the deferred 2030 date, with a methane trajectory restored to the upper end of the Zero Carbon Act's 24–47% range rather than its weakest interpretation.
- Reinstate the offshore oil & gas exploration ban and close the fast-track loophole for extractive projects in sensitive marine habitats.
- Create a network of fully protected no-take marine reserves covering at least 10% of the mainland EEZ, not only the remote Kermadec zone.
- Restore Te Mana o te Wai freshwater settings, resource regional councils to enforce nitrogen caps, and fund the riparian planting at the scale the Climate Change Commission has modelled.
- Treat Whānau Māori co-governance — Te Urewera, Whanganui River, Treaty settlements on land and fisheries — as an environmental asset rather than a political target, building on the legal-personhood precedents that Aotearoa pioneered.
- Convert the Predator Free 2050 effort into a broader indigenous-ecosystem restoration programme that integrates native afforestation, rather than continued pine monoculture on ETS credits.

## Data Confidence

**High.** Emissions, energy, protected area, and agricultural statistics are all drawn from multiple primary sources (MfE inventory, Ember, Protected Planet, OECD, Climate Change Commission) that broadly agree. Biodiversity trend data is high-quality thanks to the DOC Threat Classification System, though species-by-species Red List coverage for invertebrates and marine taxa is incomplete.

## Key Tension

A country that markets itself as "100% Pure" and legislated net-zero by 2050 still runs an economy anchored in methane-intensive dairy and sheep, and the government elected in late 2023 is actively unwinding the environmental guardrails built over the previous decade.
