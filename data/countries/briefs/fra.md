**Composite Score: 61 / 100 — YELLOW**

## Overview

France sits in an unusual position on the planetary scoreboard: a large industrial economy with one of the lowest per-capita carbon footprints in the G20 (~5.6 tCO2e), a long institutional tradition of environmental law, and a Yale EPI ranking that routinely places it inside the global top ten. Nuclear power does most of the heavy lifting — around 67% of electricity in 2025 comes from the reactor fleet, with renewables supplying roughly a quarter and fossil generation a residual 6%. Emissions have fallen ~22% over the last decade and are on a genuine downward trajectory, not a reshuffled one.

And yet France is not on track. The Haut Conseil pour le Climat, the country's independent statutory advisor, has now warned three years in a row that the pace of emissions reduction needs to roughly double — from about 2% per year to 4.5% per year — to meet the EU-wide -55% 2030 target to which France is bound. The easy wins from the historic nuclear bet are substantially booked. What remains is the politically harder terrain: decarbonising transport (now the country's largest emitting sector), retrofitting buildings, reshaping an agricultural system that is Europe's largest, and protecting a biodiversity portfolio that — thanks to New Caledonia, French Polynesia and Guiana — ranks France among the most globally consequential state stewards of nature outside the official megadiverse list.

The past two years have revealed the fragility of the coalition required to do this. A 2024 farmers' revolt produced a rapid political retreat: pesticide reduction targets under Ecophyto were suspended, and several environmental rules were rewritten under duress. Macron's minority government faces sustained pressure from the far right for whom "ecological punishment" has become a rallying cry, while climate NGOs have used the courts — most visibly in *L'Affaire du Siècle* — to keep legal pressure on.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Pace of emissions cuts (2% vs 4.5% per year).** The stock of "easy" decarbonisation — electricity — has been largely harvested. Transport (31% of emissions) and buildings (17%) now dominate, and both require household-level capital investment (EVs, heat pumps, retrofits) that the state has struggled to finance at scale. MaPrimeRénov' budgets were cut in 2025.

**2. Strict marine protection in metropolitan waters (~4% vs 10%).** France has vast overseas marine reserves in the Pacific and Indian Oceans that pull its headline 30×30 marine number above most peers. But in metropolitan France — Mediterranean, Atlantic, Channel — only around 4% of sea is under strong protection, and bottom trawling is permitted in most Natura 2000 marine sites.

**3. Pesticide intensity.** France is Europe's biggest agricultural producer and one of its most pesticide-intensive users per hectare. The Ecophyto plan targeting -50% use has now missed two successive deadlines. The 2025 suspension under farmer pressure means the 2030 target is effectively abandoned absent a political reset.

**4. Biodiversity financing.** The National Biodiversity Strategy 2030, adopted in 2023, was underfunded from the start and took a ~20% cut in the February 2026 mid-year budget. Farmland bird populations are down ~30% in 20 years and pollinator declines are severe.

**5. Forest carbon sink collapse.** France's forest carbon sink has fallen roughly 40% since 2015 due to drought, bark-beetle outbreaks, and fires. This was not in any 2015–2019 climate plan and leaves a hidden hole of tens of megatonnes in the national net-zero arithmetic.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **March 2026:** Constitutional Council upheld the *L'Affaire du Siècle* compliance order — the state has 12 months to close the cumulative emissions debt from the 2015–2018 budget overshoot or face daily penalties.
- **May 2025:** Short-haul flight ban extended to more routes where trains cover the journey in under 2h30.
- **September 2025:** France joined the High Ambition Coalition push for a binding global plastics treaty and hosted the pre-INC-5.2 ministerial in Nice.

**Negative:**

- **July 2025:** Pesticide reduction targets under Ecophyto were suspended during the farmers' revolt.
- **February 2026:** National Biodiversity Strategy budget cut ~20%.

**Neutral:**

- **November 2025:** PPE3 adopted — 6 new EPR2 reactors plus a quadrupling of solar capacity by 2035. Reads as positive on decarbonisation arithmetic, but with unresolved cost, siting, and waste questions.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If France closed its top gaps, it would:

- Double the annual pace of emissions cuts to ~4.5% per year, with a restored and expanded retrofit programme and a transport plan honest about car-dependency reduction — not only electrification.
- Reinstate and fund Ecophyto with a binding, independently monitored glidepath, paired with compensation and transition support for farmers actually moving to agroecology.
- Upgrade metropolitan marine protection to true no-take status over at least 10% of Mediterranean, Atlantic and Channel waters, including a ban on bottom trawling inside Natura 2000 sites.
- Ring-fence biodiversity financing at a level commensurate with the 2030 strategy's stated ambitions rather than treating it as budget-adjustment variable.
- Use overseas territories' extraordinary biodiversity as a diplomatic and scientific asset, with co-management and indigenous (Kanak, Polynesian, Amerindian) authority genuinely embedded rather than token.
- Let the *L'Affaire du Siècle* compliance order set a precedent: an executive that treats court-ordered climate targets as non-optional.

## Data Confidence

**High.** Emissions, energy, pollution and protected-area data are multi-source and well-documented. Biodiversity and agriculture pillars rely more on national reporting with known measurement gaps (particularly on overseas biodiversity and actual-use pesticide data).

## Key Tension

A low-carbon electricity system (nuclear-dominated) gives France genuine climate credibility, but the political coalition required to decarbonise transport, buildings and agriculture is fraying under far-right and farm-sector pressure — the easy wins are taken, the hard ones are stalling.
