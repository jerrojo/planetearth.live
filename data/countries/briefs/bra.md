# Brazil — Planet Lens Profile

**Composite Score: 52 / 100 — YELLOW**
**Last updated: 2026-04-20**

---

## Overview

Brazil is the pivot country of the global biosphere. Roughly 10% of all known species live inside its borders; the Amazon basin alone stores an estimated 120 billion tonnes of carbon, and the Cerrado — the world's most biodiverse savanna — sits on top of the aquifers that feed a continent. What Brazil chooses to protect or clear in the next decade will register on the global climate ledger more directly than almost any other national decision. That weight is why the country hosts COP30 in Belém in November 2025: the first UN climate conference ever held inside the Amazon.

The signals are moving in two directions at once. Under Lula's third administration, INPE's PRODES system recorded a 30.6% fall in Amazon deforestation in 2023–24 and a further 30.6% drop in the following reference period — the sharpest reversal since the system began. IBAMA has been refunded, FUNAI rebuilt, and the PPCDAm enforcement plan reactivated after being effectively disabled from 2019 to 2022. Meanwhile, the electricity grid is ~89% renewable, Brazil has the world's most mature ethanol fleet, and in December 2024 the country signed its regulated carbon market into law. Yet the Cerrado is disappearing faster than the Amazon in many months; the 2024 fire season was the worst since 2007; Congress is advancing a "Devastation PL" that would gut environmental licensing; and Petrobras continues pushing for oil exploration at the mouth of the Amazon. The government that cut deforestation hardest is also the one expanding the offshore fossil frontier.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Cerrado native vegetation loss (~700,000 ha/yr vs zero).** While the Amazon is the global emblem, Brazil's true deforestation frontier has shifted south and east. MapBiomas now records more native-vegetation loss in the Cerrado than in the Amazon in most months of 2024. The Cerrado has no equivalent of the Amazon's international attention, weaker legal protection under the Forest Code's 20% reserve rule (vs 80% in the Amazon), and is the primary source of Brazil's soy-export boom.

**2. 2030 emissions vs 1.5°C pathway (~64% gap).** Current policies project ~1.15 GtCO2e in 2030; a 1.5°C-compatible pathway is closer to 0.70 Gt. The NDC's 59–67% cut is technically compatible only on its more ambitious end, and only if Amazon enforcement holds AND Cerrado protection begins AND agricultural methane bends AND Petrobras does not open new basins.

**3. Indigenous Land demarcation backlog (~250 territories).** Indigenous Lands are empirically the single most effective land-tenure category for carbon retention in the Amazon, outperforming strict protected areas on deforestation rates. Roughly 250 TIs remain in the demarcation pipeline, some for over thirty years. The 2023 Marco Temporal congressional override and ongoing Supreme Court litigation have slowed the pace; Lula has signed six new decrees but the backlog is structural.

**4. Environmental defenders killed (25 in 2023, Global Witness).** Brazil is consistently among the top three deadliest countries for land and environmental defenders, with Indigenous and quilombola leaders in Pará, Rondônia, and Maranhão at the highest risk. Most killings are linked to illegal mining (garimpo), land-grabbing (grilagem), and illegal logging. The Escazú Agreement, which Brazil has signed but not ratified, would require binding protection measures.

**5. Fossil fuel subsidies (~$27B/yr explicit, IISD estimates).** Despite its clean grid, Brazil's explicit fossil subsidies are among the highest in the Global South, concentrated in diesel tax exemptions, offshore oil tax benefits (REPETRO), and gasoline price stabilisation. Petrobras' plan to drill in the Foz do Amazonas basin sits in direct tension with COP30 diplomacy.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **November 2024:** Brazil files its 2035 NDC first among major economies — 59–67% net GHG cut below 2005.
- **November 2024:** PRODES confirms Amazon deforestation down 30.6% — lowest in nine years.
- **December 2024:** Regulated carbon market law (SBCE) signed, covering facilities above 25 ktCO2e.
- **June 2025:** Six Indigenous Land demarcation decrees signed on World Environment Day.
- **October 2025:** COP30 opens in Belém; Tropical Forest Forever Facility launched as a new forest-finance instrument.

**Negative:**

- **September 2024:** Worst Pantanal and Amazon fire season since 2007 — roughly 11.8M ha burned nationally.
- **April 2025:** Senate approves the "Devastation PL" weakening environmental licensing across federal, state, and municipal levels.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Brazil closed its top gaps, it would:

- Extend the Amazon zero-deforestation trajectory to the Cerrado, Pantanal, and Caatinga with a binding PPCerrado plan and real-time MapBiomas-linked enforcement.
- Lock the 2035 NDC at its upper bound (67% cut) and cancel new offshore oil exploration in the Foz do Amazonas and Pelotas basins.
- Ratify the Escazú Agreement and implement a funded federal defenders-protection program with Indigenous and quilombola-led leadership.
- Complete demarcation of the ~250 pending Indigenous Lands inside the current presidential term, codifying the Supreme Court's rejection of the Marco Temporal thesis.
- Phase out REPETRO-style fossil tax breaks and redirect savings into a just transition for Amazonian and semi-arid communities that currently depend on extractive economies.
- Use COP30 host-year leverage to convert the Tropical Forest Forever Facility from pledge into capitalised delivery.

## Data Confidence

**Medium.** Emissions, deforestation, and protected-area data are high-confidence (INPE, MapBiomas, and Protected Planet cross-validate well). Agriculture and pollution pillars lean more on modelled estimates. The defenders and Indigenous-land figures are credible but known to undercount isolated events in remote regions.

## Key Tension

Brazil is hosting COP30 in the Amazon while its Congress greenlights new offshore oil frontiers and guts environmental licensing — the government that cut deforestation hardest is also the one betting hardest on fossil expansion.
