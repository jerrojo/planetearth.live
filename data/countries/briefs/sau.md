**Composite Score: 25 / 100 — RED**

## Overview

Saudi Arabia is the pivot point of the global fossil economy. It holds roughly 15% of proven oil reserves, is the world's largest crude oil exporter, and its national oil company — Saudi Aramco — remains the single most profitable corporation on Earth and the largest industrial source of cumulative CO2 ever traced to one company. Per-capita emissions sit at ~21 tCO2e, more than double the OECD average and more than twice China's, on a population of just 36.9 million. Total emissions of ~790 MtCO2e place the kingdom among the ten largest national emitters despite a modest population, and Climate Action Tracker rates both the 2030 NDC and overall policy "Critically insufficient" — its worst category, shared with only a handful of petrostates.

The official narrative is one of transformation. Vision 2030, launched in 2016, framed diversification away from oil. The Saudi Green Initiative pledged 50% renewable electricity by 2030 and 10 billion trees. A 2060 net zero target was announced in 2021. NEOM's Line, Trojena, and Sindalah were marketed as proof that the desert could host a post-carbon civilization. At the same time, Aramco has reversed its 2024 pause on capacity expansion and is planning to sustain or grow crude output through 2030, major gas investments are accelerating, and Saudi delegates have led the bloc that blocked fossil fuel phase-out language at COP27, diluted the UAE Consensus at COP28, and obstructed implementation language at COP29 and COP30. Saudi Arabia's "Circular Carbon Economy" framing centers CCUS and hydrogen as mitigation pathways that preserve, rather than retire, oil extraction.

Beyond climate, the country is not megadiverse but is ecologically distinctive: Arabian oryx reintroduction is a genuine conservation win, Red Sea coral reefs are globally significant, and the Asir mountains host endemic species not found anywhere else. Freshwater tells the starkest story — Saudi Arabia has near-zero renewable freshwater per capita, and roughly 60% of water supply comes from desalination plants that are themselves fossil-fuelled, creating a closed loop of hydrocarbon dependency inside the water system itself.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Emissions trajectory vs 1.5°C pathway (131% gap).** Under current policies, Saudi Arabia is projected to emit ~830 MtCO2e in 2030. A 1.5°C-compatible domestic pathway is closer to ~360 MtCO2e. Closing this gap would require the NDC to move from a BAU-referenced conditional pledge to an absolute cut of roughly half on 2019 levels, coupled with an actual phase-down of Aramco's crude capacity rather than its quiet expansion.

**2. Renewable share of electricity (3% vs 50% pledged).** Sudair (1.5 GW) was commissioned in 2025 and a meaningful PV pipeline exists, but deployment is running years behind schedule. The binding constraint is not sunshine or capital — it is that subsidized domestic fossil power makes renewables uneconomic against a tariff that does not price carbon or pass through global oil value.

**3. Fossil fuel subsidies (~$30B explicit; ~$260B implicit per IMF).** Saudi Arabia is consistently in the top three globally on IMF's implicit subsidy measure (which prices undertaxed externalities), and explicit subsidies alone exceed 2.5% of GDP. G20 members pledged in 2009 to phase these out. No credible schedule exists.

**4. Marine protected areas (5.9% vs 30%).** Red Sea coastal megaprojects — NEOM, Red Sea Global, AMAALA — are sited in globally important coral ecosystems that remain under-surveyed. Two new MPAs near Farasan in early 2026 are a positive step but the kingdom is far from 30×30 on the water.

**5. Climate governance and civil space.** Environmental defenders operate under severe constraint: independent climate journalism is effectively absent, NGOs are state-licensed, and Sabin Center lists zero domestic climate cases. Without open information and civic accountability, the Saudi Green Initiative's trees, targets, and timelines cannot be independently verified.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **September 2025:** Sudair 1.5 GW solar PV plant fully commissioned — largest utility solar in the Gulf.
- **October 2025:** Public Investment Fund issued $5B green bond for renewables and green hydrogen.
- **January 2026:** Two new Red Sea marine protected areas near Farasan Islands (~4,200 km²).

**Negative:**

- **November 2025:** At COP30, Saudi delegation resisted fossil phase-out language in final text.
- **February 2026:** Aramco reversed 2024 capacity pause — restoring plan to sustain/grow crude output above 12 mb/d.

**Neutral:**

- **December 2025:** NEOM scaled back — The Line shortened from 170 km to ~2.4 km by 2030 per leaked internal plans; Trojena and Sindalah proceeding.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Saudi Arabia closed its top gaps, it would:

- Convert the 2060 net-zero pledge into a statute with a dated, declining carbon budget, and submit a 2035 NDC with an absolute cut on 2019 levels.
- Publish an Aramco capacity glide-path — including a managed wind-down of exploration CapEx — rather than expanding sustained crude capacity.
- Hit the 50% renewable electricity pledge by 2030 and retire, not just supplement, gas capacity as solar and storage scale.
- Phase out explicit fossil subsidies on a published schedule, using Public Investment Fund surpluses to underwrite a just transition for oil-sector employment.
- Triple marine protected area coverage and require cumulative-impact assessments for all Red Sea megaprojects, with data released publicly.
- Stop using COP negotiations to dilute phase-out language — credibility cannot be bought with green bonds while the bloc position blocks the 1.5°C ceiling.

## Data Confidence

**Medium.** Emissions, subsidies, CAT rating, and protected area figures are high-confidence (multiple primary sources converge). Biodiversity, agriculture, and defenders data are lower-confidence: the near-zero count of defenders killed and climate cases filed reflects the constrained civic space more than genuine absence of environmental conflict, and independent biodiversity monitoring is sparse across the peninsula.

## Key Tension

Saudi Arabia has pledged net zero by 2060 and is building one of the world's largest utility-solar pipelines, while simultaneously expanding crude oil capacity and systematically blocking fossil fuel phase-out language in UN climate negotiations — its climate policy is structurally organized around preserving oil demand, not ending it.
