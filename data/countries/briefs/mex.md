# Mexico — Planet Lens Profile

**Composite Score: 37 / 100 — RED**
**Last updated: 2026-04-20**

---

## Overview

Mexico sits at a rare pivot point. In October 2024, Claudia Sheinbaum — an energy engineer, former IPCC contributing author, and the first climate scientist ever to lead a major economy — took office as president. In most countries this would signal a turning of the page. In Mexico it has, so far, meant a continuation. The fiscal architecture of the Mexican state is still tethered to Pemex, the national oil company whose debt exceeds $100B and whose tax contributions underwrite social programs. The inherited flagship infrastructure — the Dos Bocas refinery on the Gulf and the Maya Train looping the Yucatán — was built on environmental-review waivers and is now locked in for decades. The new administration's 2025 National Energy Plan kept private renewables capped at 46% of generation and redirected the largest share of new investment back into state-owned hydrocarbons.

Beyond climate, Mexico is among the five most biodiverse nations on Earth, hosting the monarch butterfly's overwintering forests in Michoacán, the vaquita porpoise in the Upper Gulf of California (fewer than ten individuals remain), jaguars in the Selva Maya, and an astonishing endemic flora across its deserts, cloud forests, and cenote systems. It is also, consistently, one of the three deadliest countries in the world to be an environmental defender. Global Witness recorded 18 defender killings in the most recent reporting year — overwhelmingly campesinos and Indigenous leaders resisting mining concessions, hydro projects, and agro-industrial expansion. The Escazú Agreement, which Mexico ratified in 2021, was supposed to change that. It hasn't yet.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. 2030 emissions vs 1.5°C pathway (76% gap).** Current policies put Mexico on track for roughly 790 MtCO2e in 2030. A 1.5°C-compatible pathway would require roughly 450 MtCO2e. The gap is wider, in proportional terms, than any other major Latin American economy — a consequence of transport-sector growth, continued gas-fired generation, and the refusal to unbundle CFE generation monopolies that block renewable grid access.

**2. Renewable electricity share (22% vs 60%+ needed).** Mexico has world-class solar irradiance in Sonora and Baja California and proven wind corridors in Oaxaca, yet permitting for private renewables has been effectively frozen since 2021. CFE prioritizes dispatch of its own fossil-fired plants even when cheaper renewable generation is available. Unlocking private capital would require regulatory independence that successive administrations have dismantled.

**3. Terrestrial protected areas (14.8% vs 30%).** CONANP, the national protected-areas agency, has seen its real budget fall every year since 2019. Many reserves exist on paper without rangers, signage, or active management. Reaching 30×30 will require not just new designations but restoring institutional capacity — and integrating Indigenous and community-conserved areas (ICCAs) into the formal count, which LandMark estimates could add meaningful hectares.

**4. Environmental defender killings (18/year).** Mexico has ranked in the global top three every year since 2019. Impunity is near-total: fewer than 5% of cases result in prosecution. The killings concentrate in states with large mining, hydro, and forestry concessions — Oaxaca, Chihuahua, Michoacán, and the Yucatán Peninsula. Full Escazú implementation, including the defender protection protocol, remains the single highest-leverage governance reform.

**5. Fossil fuel subsidies (~$18B explicit per IEA).** Mexico's explicit subsidies swelled under the AMLO-era gasoline price stabilization mechanism and Pemex debt absorption. The IMF's implicit subsidy estimate, which internalizes the health and climate externalities of underpriced fuels, is several times higher. Redirecting even a fraction into grid modernization would close much of the renewable gap.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **June 2025:** Supreme Court ruled against Maya Train environmental impact waivers on Tramo 5, ordering retrospective review of cenote and forest impacts.
- **November 2025:** Updated 2035 NDC submitted at COP30, stepping ambition from 35% to 45% below BAU (though CAT still rates "Insufficient").
- **February 2026:** Joint CONAPESCA–Navy enforcement operation launched in the vaquita refuge after survey confirmed fewer than ten individuals remain.

**Negative:**

- **February 2025:** National Energy Plan 2025–2030 committed $23B to Pemex and capped private renewables at 46% of generation.
- **September 2025:** Global Witness ranked Mexico the 2nd deadliest country for environmental defenders in its 2025 Defenders Report.

**Neutral:**

- **October 2024:** Sheinbaum inaugurated; first climate scientist to lead a G20 economy, but her Energy Plan continued her predecessor's hydrocarbon-first framing.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Mexico closed its top gaps, it would:

- Restore regulatory independence for CRE and CNH so private renewables can clear permitting queues, targeting 60% clean electricity by 2030.
- Publish a Pemex transition plan with a capped production trajectory and a just-transition fund for refining and Gulf-state workers.
- Triple CONANP's operating budget and co-manage 30×30 gains through recognized ICCAs, closing the terrestrial protected-areas gap.
- Operationalize the Escazú defender protocol with a dedicated prosecutor's office for crimes against defenders and binding consultation for extractive and infrastructure permits.
- Enforce the existing 2020 glyphosate phase-out and negotiate a USMCA environmental side-letter that protects native maize diversity.
- Redirect the $18B fossil subsidy envelope toward grid modernization, rural solar, and vaquita and monarch recovery programs.

## Data Confidence

**Medium.** Emissions, energy, and protected-area figures are well-triangulated across OWID, Ember, and Protected Planet. Defender-killing counts are from Global Witness and CEMDA and are likely undercounts. Biodiversity trend data for less-charismatic taxa is thinner, and the vaquita population estimate carries wide confidence intervals given how few individuals remain to survey.

## Key Tension

A climate-scientist president leads a petro-state whose fiscal survival is structurally tethered to Pemex — the test of the Sheinbaum era is whether science in the presidency can outweigh hydrocarbons in the treasury.
