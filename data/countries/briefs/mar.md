## Overview

Morocco is the rare country whose climate reputation runs ahead of its climate reality. It is one of only a handful of nations with a Climate Action Tracker rating of "Almost sufficient" — a status earned largely through low per-capita emissions (~2.5 tCO2e, roughly a quarter of the global average) and some of the most ambitious renewable-energy targets in the Global South. The Noor Ouarzazate solar complex on the edge of the Sahara is one of the world's largest concentrated solar power installations, and the 2030 National Energy Strategy commits the country to 52% renewable electricity. On top of that, the "Morocco Offer" launched in 2024 has turned the country into one of the front-runners for green hydrogen exports to Europe, with roughly 183,000 hectares now allocated to H2 and green ammonia projects destined largely for German and Dutch offtakers.

The reality on the ground is more mixed. Coal still supplies roughly 60% of electricity via the Jorf Lasfar and Safi plants, renewables sit at ~22% of generation despite a decade of megaproject announcements, and absolute emissions have climbed ~6.5% over the last five years as industry and transport expand. Beyond climate, Morocco sits in a Mediterranean Basin biodiversity hotspot rich in argan woodland, cedar forest, Barbary macaque and a critical migratory-bird funnel through the Strait of Gibraltar — but terrestrial protected areas are only ~1.5% of territory and marine only ~0.8%, among the lowest coverage in North Africa. The Atlas Mountains, struck by a devastating 6.8-magnitude earthquake in September 2023, remain under compounding pressure from drought, deforestation and reconstruction-driven land-use change.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Coal still majority of the grid (60% vs <20% needed).** The 52% renewable target for 2030 depends on explicit coal retirements that have not yet been scheduled. Safi and Jorf Lasfar remain the backbone of baseload; in July 2025 ONEE extended Safi's operating permit on grid-stability grounds. Without binding retirement dates, the renewable build-out risks supplementing coal rather than replacing it.

**2. 30-point gap to the 2030 renewable target (22% vs 52%).** To close this in five years Morocco would need to roughly triple solar and wind deployment, accelerate grid interconnection across the Atlas, and resolve land-rights questions in southern provinces where many of the largest projects sit — including within the disputed Western Sahara territory, where governance opacity makes independent monitoring difficult.

**3. Protected areas far below 30×30 (1.5% terrestrial, 0.8% marine).** Morocco endorsed the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, and the February 2026 NBSAP update commits to a 10% terrestrial milestone by 2028 — but the trajectory to 30% by 2030 is not credible without a tenfold scale-up and the formal recognition of community-conserved argan and cork-oak landscapes as OECMs.

**4. Agricultural water stress (87% of freshwater).** The Souss-Massa and Saïss basins — where berries, tomatoes and avocados are grown largely for European supermarkets — show groundwater tables falling more than a meter per year. The Generation Green strategy continues to back high-water export crops in hydrologically stressed zones. Desalination expansion (on track for 50% of drinking water by 2030) helps cities, but does not touch agricultural demand.

**5. Phosphate externalities.** OCP is the world's largest phosphate producer and reserves holder, underwriting global food security but generating phosphogypsum waste and marine effluent along the Atlantic coast at industrial scale. Full-lifecycle environmental reporting for the sector remains limited.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **March 2026:** First binding green hydrogen and green ammonia offtake agreements signed with German and Dutch consortia under the Morocco Offer.
- **December 2025:** Noor Midelt I (800 MW hybrid CSP + PV) commissioned after repeated delays.
- **February 2026:** Updated NBSAP ratified with a 2028 milestone on protected-area expansion.

**Neutral:**

- **September 2025:** Emergency Water Programme extended through 2027 with a 143 bn MAD budget — ambitious on supply, silent on agricultural demand.

**Negative:**

- **July 2025:** Safi coal plant operating permit extended on grid-stability grounds.
- **November 2025:** Souss-Massa avocado and berry acreage expansion approved over basin-agency objections.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Morocco closed its top gaps, it would:

- Publish a binding coal-retirement schedule with firm dates for Jorf Lasfar and Safi, tied to the 52% renewables target.
- Front-load renewable build-out to reach at least 35% of electricity by 2028, using grid interconnection to Europe as leverage for storage investment.
- Scale terrestrial protection from 1.5% to 15%+ by 2030 through a mix of new parks, argan-woodland biosphere recognition, and OECMs co-managed with amazigh communities.
- Recouple the Generation Green agricultural strategy to basin-level water budgets: cap export-crop acreage where aquifers are declining, and redirect subsidies toward drought-resilient staples.
- Publish lifecycle environmental data for OCP phosphate operations, including phosphogypsum management and marine effluent.
- Open civic space for environmental monitoring — including in Western Sahara's renewable zones — so civil society can co-verify progress rather than work around prosecution risk.

## Data Confidence

**Medium.** Emissions, energy-mix and protected-area data are high-confidence. Biodiversity, pollution, and governance pillars rely on partial or single-source indicators; the defenders count of zero almost certainly understates reality given documented prosecutions of activists in Jerada and the Rif. Western Sahara data overlap further reduces confidence in renewable-zone monitoring.

## Key Tension

Morocco exports the clean-energy future — green hydrogen to Europe, world-scale CSP, phosphate for global food security — while running its own grid on coal and draining its aquifers to grow berries and avocados for European supermarkets.
