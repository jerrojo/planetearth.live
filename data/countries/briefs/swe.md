## Overview

Sweden is the country the rest of the world points to when it wants to prove that decarbonization is possible without collapsing living standards. It introduced a carbon tax in 1991 — the world's earliest — and has since raised it to roughly SEK 1,500 (about USD 140) per tonne of CO2, the highest explicit carbon price on the planet. Its electricity mix is ~98% non-fossil, built on roughly 40% hydropower, 30% nuclear, 23% wind, and a sliver of solar and biomass. Per-capita emissions sit around 4.1 tCO2e, about a third of the US figure and less than half of China's. Yale EPI 2024 placed Sweden in the global top five.

And yet. Since the Kristersson coalition (Moderates, Christian Democrats, Liberals, backed by Sweden Democrats) took office in late 2022, the climate frame has shifted. The government cut the fuel tax in 2023, axed the EV climate bonus, reduced the biofuel blending mandate, and weakened organic farming support. The independent Climate Policy Council has warned three years running that statutory 2030 and 2045 targets are now out of reach under current policy — the first time in a decade Sweden is projected to miss its own climate law. Transport emissions, which had been falling, rose in 2024.

Beyond climate, the deeper story is the boreal forest. Sweden is 69% forested, but that statistic conceals a monoculture plantation landscape: even-aged spruce and pine, managed by clearcutting, with only around 9% of productive forest qualifying as old-growth or near-natural. Satellite studies show the Swedish carbon sink has weakened sharply since 2015 as intensive logging has begun to outpace growth. This is also indigenous land — Sami reindeer-herding communities are in active conflict with LKAB's mining expansion and with state forestry in the north.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. 2030 emissions vs national target (62% gap).** Sweden's own Climate Act sets a 2030 goal of -63% vs 1990 levels, roughly 26 MtCO2e. Current policies point to ~42 MtCO2e. Transport is the primary driver of the miss, followed by stalled progress in agriculture. Closing the gap requires reversing the 2023 fuel-tax cut, restoring the biofuel mandate, and electrifying heavy transport.

**2. Old-growth and near-natural forest (~9% vs halt-and-restore).** The Swedish boreal is one of Europe's largest forest carbon pools, and it is thinning. Only ~9% of productive forest is old-growth, concentrated in the mountainous north. Clearcutting is legal as young as ~60 years, and the "freedom with responsibility" model of the 1993 Forestry Act has resulted in strong production rights and weak environmental enforcement.

**3. Protected terrestrial area (15.0% vs 30%).** Sweden's protected land is concentrated in remote, low-productivity mountain terrain. The productive southern forest, where most biodiversity is under pressure, is barely protected. Reaching 30×30 requires protecting forest with economic value, which is politically harder.

**4. Protected marine area (14.6% vs 30%).** The Baltic Sea is one of the most stressed seas in Europe — eutrophic, with cod stocks collapsed. Sweden's Baltic MPAs are many but lightly managed; effective no-take zones are rare.

**5. Red List trajectory (~2,000 species, ~1 in 5 assessed).** Around 1,400 red-listed species depend on forest habitats that intensive forestry continues to erode. Without forest reform, this list grows.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **June 2025:** HYBRIT fossil-free steel joint venture (SSAB/LKAB/Vattenfall) begins commercial-scale shipments — a flagship heavy-industry decarbonization milestone.
- **September 2025:** Autumn budget restores a reduced climate bonus for EVs after a two-year slump in EV sales.

**Negative:**

- **March 2026:** Climate Policy Council's 2026 annual report again concludes statutory 2030 and 2045 targets will be missed under current policy.
- **November 2025:** LKAB's Per Geijer rare-earth project gets environmental permit; Sami communities appeal on FPIC grounds.
- **December 2024:** Supreme Administrative Court greenlights expanded spruce logging in reindeer-herding areas over Sami objections.

**Neutral:**

- **February 2025:** Cap on new nuclear reactors removed and state subsidies extended; critics argue the signal crowds out wind investment.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Sweden closed its top gaps, it would:

- Reinstate and strengthen the fuel tax, biofuel blending mandate, and EV climate bonus — and let the carbon tax rise on schedule.
- Rewrite the Forestry Act to give environmental goals genuine parity with production, mandate continuous-cover forestry options, and set a hard floor on old-growth protection.
- Double terrestrial and marine protected area coverage by 2030, focused on productive southern forest and on no-take Baltic zones.
- Adopt statutory FPIC (Free, Prior, and Informed Consent) for projects on Sami traditional territory, consistent with ILO 169 which Sweden has still not ratified.
- Restore organic farming subsidies and tighten Baltic nutrient-runoff standards.
- Treat the Climate Policy Council's warnings as binding triggers for corrective action, not as annual rituals to be ignored.

## Data Confidence

**High.** Emissions, energy, protected area, air quality, and governance indicators are cross-confirmed by OWID, Ember, EEA, Protected Planet, and Yale EPI. Biodiversity and agriculture pillars rely more heavily on single national sources (Artdatabanken, Naturvårdsverket), and forest condition data has genuine methodological dispute between government and independent researchers.

## Key Tension

Sweden's carbon tax and near-fossil-free grid are the template the world aspires to — but intensive boreal forestry, a weakening carbon sink, and a government actively pulling back on climate policy are eroding the headline story from the inside.
