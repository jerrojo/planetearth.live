# Canada — Planet Lens Profile

**Composite Score: 51 / 100 — YELLOW**
**Last updated: 2026-04-20**

## Overview

Canada is a country whose environmental story splits along a fault line between its diplomacy and its geology. It hosted COP15 in Montreal where the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework was adopted, it legislated net-zero by 2050 in the 2021 Net-Zero Emissions Accountability Act, and it has one of the cleanest electricity grids in the G7 — about 68% renewable (mostly hydro) plus 15% nuclear. Yet it also sits on the world's third-largest proven oil reserves, almost entirely locked in the Alberta bitumen sands, and it remains the highest per-capita emitter in the G7 at roughly 17.2 tonnes CO2e per person. The annual inventory showed emissions declining ~7% over the past five years, and the Carney government that took office after the March 2025 federal election has kept the industrial carbon price and the oil & gas emissions cap intact — but it also scrapped the consumer carbon price that had become politically toxic, and Bill C-5 (Major Projects Act) passed in June 2025 to accelerate approvals for pipelines, transmission, and critical-minerals mines.

The boreal forest adds a second layer of paradox. Canada stewards roughly a quarter of the world's remaining intact boreal, a globally decisive carbon sink and a homeland for hundreds of First Nations, Métis, and Inuit communities. Yet the 2023 wildfire season burned 18.5 million hectares — more than double any previous record in Canadian history — and 2024 continued at extreme levels. By the country's own accounting, the managed forest has shifted from net sink to net source in many recent years. The Arctic, meanwhile, is warming roughly three times the global average, with permafrost thaw and sea-ice loss now visible in annual Indigenous land-use surveys.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. 2030 emissions vs 1.5°C pathway (67% gap).** Current-policies projections put Canada at roughly 600 MtCO2e in 2030; a 1.5°C-compatible share would be around 360 Mt. Closing the gap requires the oil & gas cap to bite as written, the industrial carbon price to continue rising past 2030, and a clear retirement schedule for the remaining gas-fired generation on the Alberta and Saskatchewan grids.

**2. Per-capita emissions (~17.2 tCO2e, 244% above a 1.5°C-consistent level).** Long cold winters, long driving distances, and an energy-export economy all structurally inflate Canadian per-capita emissions — but so do building-code gaps, truck-heavy transport, and the absence of a national intercity rail strategy. No G7 peer has as large a gap to close per person.

**3. Terrestrial protected areas (13.6% vs 30%).** Indigenous Protected and Conserved Areas (IPCAs) are now the primary engine of expansion — Thaidene Nëné, proposed expansions across the Northwest Territories and Nunavut — but federal-provincial coordination in Ontario, Quebec, and British Columbia remains the binding constraint.

**4. Marine protected areas (14.7% vs 30%).** Canada has the world's longest coastline and three ocean basins. Progress has been faster here than on land, but most protections do not meet IUCN "strict" criteria, and fisheries management in the Atlantic (especially right-whale habitat) and the Arctic is uneven.

**5. Fossil fuel subsidies and public financing (~$18.5B/yr per IISD).** Canada formally ended "inefficient" fossil subsidies in 2023 under a new federal framework, but public financing through Export Development Canada and other Crown corporations continues at a scale that dwarfs explicit budget items. This is the single most consequential unfinished item from the G20 2009 commitment.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **November 2024:** Oil and Gas Emissions Cap regulations finalized — sector-specific cap declining to 35–38% below 2019 by 2030.
- **August 2024:** Clean Electricity Regulations finalized, targeting a net-zero grid by 2035 (with flexibility provisions for gas peakers that critics flag).
- **October 2024:** Thaidene Nëné IPCA expanded; further Indigenous-led designations advanced in NWT and Nunavut.

**Negative:**

- **April 2025:** Carney government repealed the consumer carbon price ("fuel charge") under election-cycle pressure.
- **June 2025:** Bill C-5 (Major Projects Act) passed to fast-track pipeline, transmission, and mining approvals; Indigenous nations have filed a constitutional challenge citing UNDRIP obligations.

**Neutral:**

- **February 2025:** 2035 NDC submitted at UNFCCC — 45–50% below 2005 — judged insufficient for a 1.5°C share by Climate Action Tracker.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If Canada closed its top gaps, it would:

- Harden the oil and gas emissions cap against provincial legal challenge and refuse new export-oriented bitumen infrastructure beyond projects already in service.
- Raise the 2035 NDC to at least 60% below 2005 and publish a sectoral pathway that shows how freight and buildings — not just electricity — contribute.
- Scale IPCAs as the backbone of 30×30, resourcing Indigenous Guardians programs at a level commensurate with the conservation work they deliver.
- Reinstate a consumer-side carbon price with dividend visibility or equivalent climate-income mechanism so the political economy stabilizes rather than oscillates.
- Wind down Export Development Canada's fossil portfolio, moving the capital into grid interties, heat-pump deployment, and critical-minerals processing with Indigenous equity.
- Rebuild the national fire and forest-carbon monitoring system to match the new wildfire regime — including honest inventory accounting that treats the managed forest as the carbon liability it has become.

## Data Confidence

**High.** Emissions, energy, forest, and protected-area data are all multi-source and regularly audited (ECCC National Inventory Report, Natural Resources Canada, Protected Planet, Ember). Biodiversity pillar is medium-confidence — species-level Red List data is solid, but population-trajectory data for boreal caribou and Pacific salmon varies by province. The "defenders killed" indicator reads zero, consistent with Global Witness methodology, but understates pressure on Indigenous land defenders who have faced injunctions, arrests, and surveillance (Wet'suwet'en, Fairy Creek).

## Key Tension

Canada brokered the global 30×30 biodiversity deal and legislated net-zero, yet remains the G7's highest per-capita emitter and its largest untapped bitumen reserve — a country whose climate diplomacy is credible only to the extent it can outpace the political gravity of the Alberta oil sands.
