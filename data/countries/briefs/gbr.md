# United Kingdom — Planet Lens Profile

**Composite Score: 53 / 100 — YELLOW**
**Last updated: 2026-04-20**

---

## Overview

The United Kingdom occupies an unusual position in the planetary ledger: a climate leader sitting on top of a nature catastrophe. Emissions have fallen more than 50% from their 1990 baseline — the deepest cut of any major G20 economy — and on 30 September 2024 the last coal-fired power station, Ratcliffe-on-Soar, shut its boilers, ending 142 years of coal generation and making Britain the first G7 nation to fully leave the fuel that powered the Industrial Revolution it invented. The Labour government that took office in July 2024 has moved faster than most analysts expected: lifting the de facto ban on onshore wind in England, establishing the publicly-owned Great British Energy via the 2025 Act, and submitting an 81%-by-2035 NDC at COP29 that Climate Action Tracker judges 'Almost sufficient' — the highest rating of any major economy.

But beneath this relatively healthy climate story sits a biosphere in free-fall. The State of Nature 2023 report concluded that one in six UK species is at risk of national extinction and species abundance has fallen roughly 19% since 1970. On the Biodiversity Intactness Index the UK sits in the bottom 10% globally at around 50% — the most nature-depleted country in the G7, and one of the most depleted in the world. The chalk streams, of which the UK holds roughly 85% of the planetary total, are strangled by sewage and agricultural runoff. Only 14% of English rivers reach 'good' ecological status. Water companies discharged raw sewage into rivers and coastal waters for 3.6 million hours in 2024 — a 54% increase year-on-year. This is the paradox: a country close to solving its power-sector emissions while its rivers, soils, woodlands, and wildlife continue their long collapse.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Biodiversity intactness (BII ~50% vs ~90% safe limit).** The UK is in the bottom decile globally on the Natural History Museum's Biodiversity Intactness Index. Centuries of enclosure, agricultural intensification, and urbanisation have left the country without the intact ecosystems its peers retain. Closing this gap is a multi-decade rewilding programme, not a 5-year deliverable, and it currently has no credible political owner.

**2. Effectively protected land (~3% vs 30% by 2030).** On paper the UK protects 28% of its land and 38% of its seas. In practice, Wildlife & Countryside Link's assessment finds only around 3% of land and under 1% of sea are effectively managed for nature — the rest are paper parks where intensive grazing, dredging, bottom-trawling, and grouse-moor burning continue inside protected boundaries. The GBF 30x30 commitment is formally accepted but lacks a delivery mechanism.

**3. 2030 emissions vs NDC (22% gap).** The 68%-by-2030 headline looks aligned, but the CCC's 2025 Progress Report flags the 6th Carbon Budget as off-track, with buildings (heat pumps, retrofit) and transport (EV rollout, aviation) as the primary delivery risks. Current policies put the UK on roughly 320 MtCO2e in 2030 against a pledge of ~250 MtCO2e.

**4. River water quality (14% vs 100%).** The Water Framework Directive target that 100% of English rivers reach 'good' ecological status by 2027 will be missed by an order of magnitude. Agricultural runoff, combined sewer overflows, and underinvestment by privatised water companies drive the failure. The 2025 sewage data (3.6m hours of spills, +54% YoY) shows the trend worsening, not improving.

**5. Tree planting (~21,000 ha vs 30,000 ha/yr).** Planting rates have consistently undershot the 30,000 ha/yr target set for 2025 — a target already modest relative to what UK carbon budgets and peatland restoration imply. Scotland carries most of the effort; England has not moved the dial.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **September 2024:** Ratcliffe-on-Soar closes — UK becomes first G7 country to phase out coal power.
- **July 2024:** Labour government ends 9-year de facto onshore wind ban in England.
- **November 2024:** 81%-by-2035 NDC submitted at COP29 — among the most ambitious headline targets globally.
- **May 2025:** Great British Energy Act receives Royal Assent; £8.3bn publicly-owned clean power company operational.
- **January 2025:** Court of Session rules Rosebank and Jackdaw oil & gas approvals unlawful for failing to consider Scope 3 emissions, following the 2024 Supreme Court Finch judgment.

**Negative:**

- **September 2025:** Environment Agency confirms 3.6 million hours of raw sewage discharges in 2024 (+54% YoY).
- **November 2025:** Climate Change Committee 2025 Progress Report finds the 6th Carbon Budget off-track in buildings and transport.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If the UK closed its top gaps, it would:

- Publish a statutory Nature Recovery Strategy with binding milestones for biodiversity intactness, funded at the scale of the climate transition (currently Defra's nature budget is a fraction of DESNZ's clean power allocation).
- Convert 'paper' protected areas into effectively-managed ones by banning bottom-trawling in Marine Protected Areas, ending burning on protected peatlands, and reforming grouse-moor management.
- Accelerate building retrofit and heat-pump deployment to put the 6th Carbon Budget back on track — the single highest-leverage domestic action.
- Resolve the sewage crisis by restructuring water regulation (Ofwat reform, special administration for failing companies) and enforcing permit conditions already on the books.
- Close the new-licensing contradiction: accept the Supreme Court Finch ruling's implications and stop issuing new North Sea oil & gas licences, as the Labour manifesto pledged but has applied inconsistently.
- Hit and exceed the 30,000 ha/yr tree-planting target with native species on appropriate land, paired with a serious peatland restoration programme.

## Data Confidence

**High.** The UK has mature independent monitoring (CCC, OEP, JNCC, BTO, Natural History Museum) and data on emissions, energy, protected areas, species, and water quality is transparent and multi-source. Remaining uncertainty is concentrated in agriculture (ammonia, soil carbon) and in translating 'paper' protection into effective protection.

## Key Tension

The UK leads the G7 on decarbonising its power system — and trails it on the state of its nature; the emissions curve bends while chalk streams, rivers, and wildlife abundance continue to collapse.
