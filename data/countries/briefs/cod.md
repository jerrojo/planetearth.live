## Overview

The Democratic Republic of the Congo is the hinge country of the global carbon cycle nobody talks about. Sitting beneath its borders — in the Cuvette Centrale — is a peatland complex holding roughly 30 gigatonnes of carbon, the equivalent of three years of global CO2 emissions. Above that peat sits the Congo Basin rainforest, the second-largest contiguous tropical forest on Earth after the Amazon, of which DRC contains about sixty percent. Together these two ecosystems make DRC one of the most consequential climate countries on the planet — not because of what it emits, but because of what it stores.

The tension is that DRC is also one of the poorest countries on Earth. GDP per capita is under US$700. Roughly 60% of the population lives in extreme poverty. Sixty-five percent of electricity access is missing. Charcoal is the dominant cooking fuel. The country sits on vast cobalt and copper reserves — the critical minerals underwriting the global energy transition — and in 2022 the government auctioned thirty oil-and-gas blocks, including parcels that overlapped Virunga National Park and the Cuvette Centrale peatlands. A partial reversal in 2025 cancelled several of the most sensitive blocks, but the regulatory trajectory has not closed.

Biodiversity in DRC is civilisational in scale. The country is home to mountain and eastern lowland (Grauer's) gorillas, bonobos (endemic — they live nowhere else), okapi (endemic), forest elephants, and a plant flora running into the tens of thousands of species. Four UNESCO World Heritage sites — Virunga, Salonga, Kahuzi-Biega, Okapi Wildlife Reserve — anchor the protected-area system. All four have spent time on the "in danger" list. Virunga's rangers are among the most frequently killed environmental professionals in the world.

## Top 5 Gaps

**1. Primary forest loss (100% gap to Glasgow pledge).** DRC lost roughly 600,000 hectares of primary humid forest in 2024 per Global Forest Watch — the second-highest in the world after Brazil. Drivers are diffuse and structural: shifting cultivation around expanding road networks, charcoal to supply Kinshasa and other cities, small-scale artisanal logging, and the slow advance of mining and plantation frontiers. Unlike Brazil, DRC's loss is not dominated by large ranches that a single policy can target — it is a million small fires and chainsaws.

**2. Cuvette Centrale peatlands at extractive risk.** The Cuvette Centrale is a globally unique carbon bank, holding roughly 30 GtC in saturated peat. A partial drain-out via industrial drilling, agriculture, or even road infrastructure could release carbon at a scale comparable to several years of China's emissions. Nine of the 30 oil blocks auctioned in 2022 overlapped peatlands or Virunga; most were cancelled or re-scoped in 2025, but the precedent is set and the revenue incentive has not disappeared.

**3. Protected terrestrial area (14.2% vs 30%).** Expansion on paper is feasible — DRC has more intact forest landscape than almost any other country — but the binding constraint is not designation, it is *protection*: armed groups operate inside several existing parks, and ranger funding is chronically short.

**4. NDC finance gap (~90% short).** The DRC NDC is explicit that 21% of the 23% total reduction is conditional on international finance of roughly US$48.7 billion cumulative through 2030. Current inflows — CAFI, bilateral climate finance, some REDD+ results-based payments — run under US$1 billion per year combined. Without a step-change, the conditional targets are aspirational.

**5. Environmental defenders.** Global Witness and park-authority records point to ten to fifteen ranger deaths per year in Virunga alone, with broader land-defender violence poorly documented. DRC has not adopted Escazú-equivalent protections, and journalists covering mining-related environmental abuses face intimidation.

## Recent Actions (last 12 months)

**Positive:**

- **July 2025:** Government restructured the 2022 oil-and-gas auction, cancelling several Cuvette Centrale peat blocks after sustained civil society and donor pressure.
- **November 2025:** CAFI partnership renewed at COP30 with an additional US$150M tranche tied to verified deforestation-reduction milestones.
- **March 2026:** Ministry of Environment republished the REDD+ registry and reconfirmed the 2002 logging moratorium.

**Negative:**

- **September 2025:** Global Forest Watch confirmed 2024 primary forest loss rose again; DRC remains second globally.
- **February 2026:** M23 offensive in North Kivu forced evacuation of Virunga staff; rangers killed in subsequent weeks.

**Neutral:**

- **December 2025:** EU–DRC critical minerals strategic partnership signed, linking cobalt exports to environmental and social safeguards — the outcome depends on enforcement.

## What a Green Pathway Would Look Like

If DRC closed its top gaps, it would:

- Formally and permanently exclude the Cuvette Centrale peatlands and all UNESCO sites from oil, gas, and large-scale mining concessions — written into law, not left to ministerial discretion.
- Convert the 2002 logging moratorium into a permanent statutory ban on new industrial concessions, while massively scaling community-forestry title (CFCLs) as the legitimate alternative.
- Scale domestic clean cooking (LPG, efficient stoves, electric in grid-connected urban areas) to reduce charcoal pressure — the single largest driver of peri-urban deforestation.
- Secure results-based REDD+ finance at an order-of-magnitude higher level, tied to verified primary forest retention, with a substantial share flowing directly to communities through CFCLs.
- Formally recognise Indigenous and local community land rights — currently ~2% — at scale; evidence globally shows community-titled forest loses carbon more slowly than state-protected forest.
- Fund and professionalise park rangers, and adopt Escazú-style legal protections for environmental defenders.

## Data Confidence

**Medium.** Forest loss, emissions-from-LULUCF, and protected area coverage are high-confidence (Global Forest Watch, OWID, Protected Planet agree). Pollution and biodiversity pillars rely on single-source indicators and almost certainly undercount due to conflict-affected data collection in the east. Defender fatalities are systematically underreported globally, and DRC is no exception.

## Key Tension

DRC holds the planet's second-largest rainforest and 30 GtC of peat carbon — but with 60% of its people in extreme poverty and a cobalt/oil revenue pipeline in front of it, "leave it in the ground" only works if the rest of the world pays for the alternative.
